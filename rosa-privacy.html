Rosa — Privacy Policy

Effective date: 17 August 2026 Version: 1.0 Applies to: the Rosa iOS application and the Rosa service

1. The short version

Rosa is an AI phone receptionist. When someone rings your business and you cannot pick up, the call diverts to a phone number we give you, and Rosa answers in your business name, has a conversation with the caller, and turns that conversation into a lead card in your app.

That means Rosa handles two very different groups of people's information:

  1. You — the business owner. You signed up, you agreed to our Terms, and you chose to use Rosa. We hold your account details, your business profile, and your subscription status.
  2. Your callers — members of the public. They rang your business. They never signed up for anything and never agreed to our Terms. Their voice is recorded, their words are transcribed, and an AI writes a summary of what they said. This is the most sensitive thing Rosa does, and Section 6 of this policy is dedicated to it. If you are a caller and you are reading this, go straight to Section 6.

We do not sell personal data. We do not use it for advertising. We do not track you across other companies' apps or websites, and Rosa contains no advertising identifier.

4.2 Your phone contacts

The exclusion list ("skip the AI") works on phone numbers. It does not require access to your address book — you can type numbers in by hand, and that is the primary way it works.

Rosa never asks for permission to read your contacts, and iOS never shows you a contacts permission prompt for Rosa. That picker runs outside Rosa: it shows you your address book, you choose one person, and iOS hands Rosa only that person's name and phone number. Rosa cannot see the rest of your address book at any point, and there is no permission for you to grant or withdraw.

  • We do not upload your address book. Not any part of it, not in hashed form, not "just for matching". This is not a promise about restraint — the app is built so that it never has the access in the first place.

5. Call recording and AI disclosure

Every call answered by Rosa is answered by an artificial voice, and every call is recorded and transcribed.

5.1 What the caller hears

Rosa's greeting is the first thing on the call, before any conversation happens. It always contains three things, in this order:

  1. The business name — "you've reached [Business Name]";
  2. A clear statement that the caller is speaking to an AI, not a person — "I'm an AI assistant";
  3. A clear statement that the call is being recorded — "this call is recorded so nothing gets missed".

The greeting is one server-controlled template with the business name substituted into it. For a business called Smith Plumbing it plays as:

> "Hi, you've reached Smith Plumbing. I'm an AI assistant and this call is > recorded so nothing gets missed. How can I help?"

A business owner cannot turn the AI disclosure or the recording announcement off. The business name and the conversational style are configurable. The disclosure is not. It is a compliance control, not a preference.

If a caller asks at any point whether they are speaking to a machine or a real person, Rosa is built to answer truthfully and immediately. It will never claim to be human.

5.2 Why calls are recorded

Recordings and transcripts exist so that:

  • the AI can understand what the caller wants and respond in real time;
  • the business owner can hear exactly what was said, rather than relying on a summary;
  • a lead can be extracted accurately, including the caller's name, address and problem;
  • disputes about what was agreed on a call can be resolved;
  • we can investigate faults, abuse and suspected fraudulent or nuisance calls.

Under UK law, a business may record its own telephone calls for purposes including quality, record-keeping, compliance and resolving disputes, under the Telecommunications (Lawful Business Practice) (Interception of Communications) Regulations 2000 — but recording also has to satisfy UK GDPR separately. That is why every call carries a spoken announcement, why recordings are deleted on a schedule, and why Section 6 exists.

5.3 If you do not want to be recorded

If you are a caller and you do not want to be recorded, hang up when you hear the announcement. You can contact the business by another route — text, email, or ringing back and asking to speak to a person. If you have already spoken to Rosa and want the recording deleted, see Section 6.5.

6. Callers — people who ring a business using Rosa

This section is for members of the public who telephoned a business and were answered by Rosa. You never signed up for Rosa and never agreed to our terms. This section explains plainly what happened to what you said.

6.1 What is captured

When you ring a business that uses Rosa and the business does not answer, your call diverts to Rosa. From that call we capture:

  • Your phone number (caller ID), and the time, date and length of the call.
  • An audio recording of the whole call — your actual voice.
  • A full written transcript of the call.
  • Anything you said during the call. In practice this usually includes your name, your address, your phone number, what has gone wrong, how urgent it is, when you are available, and sometimes practical details about getting into your property (for example "the side gate is unlocked"). It is whatever you chose to say.
  • A structured summary produced by an AI from the transcript: a title, a short summary, an urgency rating, the address, your name and your contact details, pulled out into fields so the business can act on them.
  • If you gave a postcode or part of an address, that postcode or address fragment is sent to an address-lookup service during the call so Rosa can confirm the full address with you.

We also make a technical check on your phone number with our telephony provider to work out what kind of line it is (mobile, landline, VoIP). This helps us filter out automated spam calls. It does not tell us who you are.

Please do not tell Rosa anything you would not want written down. Rosa does not ask for payment card details, passwords, bank details, medical information or any other sensitive information, and it is not designed to handle them. If you volunteer something sensitive anyway — for example a health condition that explains why a repair is urgent — it will end up in the recording and the transcript along with everything else.

Rosa is not an emergency service. If you have an emergency, hang up and ring 999, or your gas, water or electricity emergency line.

6.2 Who is responsible for your data — the honest answer

This matters, so we will not hide it in legal language.

Our position is that the business you rang is the data controller for your call, and we are its data processor. The business chose to divert its phone line to Rosa, chose the questions you were asked, and decides what happens to the lead afterwards. We handle your call on that business's instructions and under a written contract with it. Under UK GDPR that makes the business responsible for telling you how your data is used and for having a lawful basis to record you, and it makes us responsible for handling the data securely and only as instructed.

We also have to be straight with you about where that is not a clean answer. We design the service: we decide that calls are recorded at all, we choose the AI providers, we set the default retention periods, and we run the AI that summarises what you said. To that extent we are making decisions about your data that the business does not make. A regulator or a court could conclude that we and the business are joint controllers, or that we are an independent controller for some of it. We take that possibility seriously rather than asserting it away.

Practically, this means: you can come to either of us. If you contact us about a call, we will act on your request where we can, and where we need the business's instruction we will pass your request on and tell you we have done so. We will not use "we're only the processor" to leave you with nowhere to go.

Separately from the calls themselves, we act as a controller in our own right for a narrow set of things: keeping the service secure, preventing abuse and fraudulent or nuisance calls, meeting our own legal obligations, and keeping aggregate, non-identifying statistics about how the system performs. Our lawful basis for those is legitimate interests (Art. 6(1)(f)) and, where applicable, legal obligation (Art. 6(1)(c)).

6.3 What the lawful basis is

For recording, transcribing and summarising your call, the lawful basis relied on is legitimate interests (UK GDPR Art. 6(1)(f)) — the interests of the business in not losing your enquiry, in having an accurate record of what you asked for, and in being able to ring you back. We consider that this is balanced by the fact that you are told at the start of the call, in plain words, that you are speaking to an AI and that the call is recorded, and by the fact that recordings are deleted on a schedule.

If the details you gave include information that counts as special category data under Art. 9 — for example anything about your health — that has a higher bar. Rosa never asks for it. Section 18 flags this as an area requiring specific legal advice before launch.

6.4 Who your call is shared with

Your recording, transcript and summary are visible to the business you rang. They are also processed by the suppliers listed in Section 7 — the telephony provider that carries the call, the voice AI platform that conducts the conversation, the AI model that writes the summary, the address-lookup service, and the database where it is all stored. Some of these suppliers process data outside the United Kingdom, including in the United States. Section 7.2 explains this.

We do not sell your data. We do not give it to advertisers. We do not use your call to train general-purpose AI models.

If the business you rang is on our Pro plan, it may also use Rosa to send you an SMS follow-up or to ring you back from the Rosa number. Those messages come from the business, not from us.

6.5 How to get a copy of your call, or have it deleted

You have the same rights as anyone else under UK GDPR (Section 10). To use them about a call:

Email mert@studionext.co.uk with:

  • the phone number you rang from,
  • the number you rang (or the name of the business), and
  • roughly when you called.

We need those three things to find your call — we have no other way to identify you. We will ask you to confirm you control that phone number before we hand over a recording of it, since a recording of a call is exactly the sort of thing that should not be released to the wrong person.

We will respond within one month. If your request is complex we may extend that by up to two further months and will tell you if we do. Where the request needs the business's instruction as controller, we will forward it to them and tell you that we have.

You can also go directly to the business you rang. Both routes are valid.

6.6 What the business owner must do

If you are a Rosa customer, this part is your obligation, not ours. Rosa gives your callers the disclosure at the start of the call, but that is not the whole of your duty. You are responsible for:

  • Making sure you have a lawful basis to record and process your callers' data — in practice, documenting a Legitimate Interests Assessment for call recording.
  • Telling your callers how you use their data, in your own privacy notice. If your business has a website, it needs a privacy notice covering call recording, AI answering and how long you keep leads. Pointing at this policy is not enough — this policy covers what we do; you need to cover what you do.
  • Being registered with the ICO if you are required to be. Most businesses that record calls are.
  • Handling requests from your callers about their data, and telling us promptly if you receive one that we need to action.
  • Not putting anything into your greeting, questions or business profile that is untrue, misleading, or that suggests to a caller that Rosa is a human being.
  • Not using the recordings, transcripts or leads for anything the caller would not reasonably expect — in particular, not adding callers to marketing lists without a proper basis under PECR.

Our Terms of Service set these out as contractual obligations. If you do not meet them, you — not us — are exposed.

7.2 Transfers outside the UK

Some of your data, and some of your callers' data, is processed outside the United Kingdom — including in the United States and the European Union. The clearest example is the voice platform that runs the live voice conversation: it is US-based and its own published GDPR information states that transfers are primarily to the United States. That means the live audio of a UK call, and its transcript, transit a US processor.

Where data leaves the UK, we rely on the transfer mechanisms permitted by UK GDPR — the UK International Data Transfer Agreement, or the EU Standard Contractual Clauses with the UK International Data Transfer Addendum, or an adequacy decision (including the UK Extension to the EU–US Data Privacy Framework where the supplier is certified under it).

This is an area we are actively working on and it is flagged in Section 18. We would rather tell you that plainly than imply a level of formality we have not yet completed.

9. Security

  • All data is encrypted in transit (TLS) and at rest.
  • Recordings are held in private storage that is not publicly reachable by URL.
  • Access to a business's data is enforced at the database level by row-level security, so one business cannot read another's calls, leads or contacts.
  • Access to production systems by our own staff is limited to the people who need it, and is logged.
  • We use reputable infrastructure providers and keep our dependencies patched.
  • Notification messages are written so that caller details are not exposed on your lock screen.

No system is perfectly secure, and we will not claim otherwise. If you think you have found a security problem in Rosa, please email mert@studionext.co.uk and we will take it seriously.

If there is a data breach that is likely to result in a risk to people's rights and freedoms, we will report it to the ICO within 72 hours of becoming aware of it, as required by Art. 33 UK GDPR. If it is likely to result in a high risk to you, we will tell you directly and without undue delay, and tell you what happened, what it means for you and what we are doing about it. Where we are acting as a processor for a business owner, we will notify that business owner without undue delay so they can meet their own obligations.

11. Children

Rosa is a business tool for adults. It is not intended for anyone under 18, and you may not create a Rosa account if you are under 18. We do not knowingly collect data from children in the app. If we learn that an account belongs to someone under 18, we will close it and delete the data.

We cannot control who telephones a business. A child may ring a business that uses Rosa, and if they do, their call is recorded and transcribed in the same way as anyone else's. If you are a parent or guardian and a child has been recorded, email mert@studionext.co.uk and we will delete the recording and transcript.
